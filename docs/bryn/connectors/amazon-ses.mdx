Amazon Simple Email Service (SES) is the AWS service for sending email from
your own domain. Bryn connects to SES as an **Output** connector: you give
Bryn an AWS access key, the region your sender lives in, a verified From
address, and a To address, and Bryn sends through your SES account rather
than its own.

SES supports one mode in Bryn: Output, for email.

<Note>
  Plays don't send email through Amazon SES yet. You can connect and
  verify an SES account today, but the email step in a Play delivers
  through Bryn's built-in email output instead. This page covers what the
  SES connection stores, how Bryn checks it, and what a send looks like.
</Note>

## How Bryn integrates Amazon SES

The connection uses an AWS access key pair, not OAuth. You set it up under
**Integrations** in the Bryn dashboard, in a form with five fields:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Access key ID** | The access key ID of an IAM user or role in the AWS account that owns your SES identities |
| **Secret access key** | The matching secret access key |
| **Region** | The AWS region where your sender identity is verified, for example `us-east-1` |
| **From address** | The address mail is sent from. It must be a verified identity in SES in that region |
| **To address** | The single address this connection delivers to, for example a team inbox |

Bryn stores the key pair as a secret and keeps the region, From address and
To address as ordinary settings. The Bryn dashboard lists the connection by
its From address, with the To address and region underneath.

Each connection is one route: one region, one From address, one To address.
To send to a second inbox, add a second connection. Saving the same region,
From and To again replaces the stored key on the existing connection
instead of creating a duplicate, and sets it back to unverified until it
is checked again. Disconnecting a connection removes the stored key once no
other connection uses it.

### What the access key needs

Bryn makes two SES calls with your key:

- `ses:GetSendQuota`, to check the connection.
- `ses:SendEmail`, to send a message.

Give the key a policy that allows those two actions and nothing else. A
dedicated IAM user for Bryn keeps it easy to rotate or revoke without
affecting anything else in your AWS account.

### How Bryn verifies the connection

Right after you connect, Bryn reads your account's sending quota in the
region you entered. This is a read-only check: it sends no email. If AWS
accepts the key, the Bryn dashboard shows **Connection verified**. If not,
it shows **The connection could not be verified.** and you can correct the
details and check again.

<Warning>
  The check confirms that your key works in that region. It does not
  confirm that your From address is verified in SES, or that your account
  is out of the SES sandbox. A connection can show as verified and still
  have its sends rejected by SES until both are in place.
</Warning>

## Output

When Bryn sends through an SES connection, the message is:

- **From** the From address on the connection.
- **To** the To address on the connection, and only that address.
- **Plain text**, with a subject line and a text body.

SES enforces its own rules on every send, so get these right in your AWS
account first:

- **Verified sender.** SES only sends from an email address or domain you
  have verified as an identity, in the same region you give Bryn. Verifying
  the whole domain lets you use any address on it.
- **Sandbox.** New SES accounts start in the sandbox, where you can only
  send to verified addresses and at a low daily volume. Either verify the
  To address as well, or request production access so SES will deliver to
  any recipient.
- **Sending limits.** Your account's daily quota and per-second rate apply
  to everything you send through SES, Bryn included.

### Set up the connection

<Steps>
  <Step title="Verify your sender in SES">
    In the SES console, pick the region you want to send from and create a
    verified identity for your domain or From address. While your account
    is in the sandbox, verify the To address too.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create an access key">
    In IAM, create a user (or use an existing one) with a policy that
    allows `ses:GetSendQuota` and `ses:SendEmail`, then create an access
    key for it. Copy the secret access key now: AWS only shows it once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect in Bryn">
    Under **Integrations** in the Bryn dashboard, add **Amazon SES** and
    fill in the access key ID, secret access key, region, From address and
    To address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the result">
    Bryn checks the key as soon as you connect. Once the dashboard shows
    **Connection verified**, the connection is ready.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**You'll know it worked when** the connection is listed under
**Integrations** with your From address, To address and region, and shows
as verified. If verification fails, check that the access key is active,
that its policy allows `ses:GetSendQuota`, and that the region matches the
one your identity is verified in.
