HubSpot is a CRM and marketing platform: the contacts, companies and deals
your team works on live there. Bryn works with HubSpot in three modes:
**Input**, where a HubSpot workflow sends Bryn an event when a contact or
company meets its trigger, **Enrichment**, where Bryn reads your HubSpot to
learn which company, owner and open deals sit behind an account, and
**Output**, where Bryn uses the same connection to act in your HubSpot.

Bryn uses your own HubSpot portal and your own token, so every read and
write counts against your HubSpot API limits.

## How Bryn integrates HubSpot

You connect HubSpot with an access token under
[**Integrations**](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations) in the Bryn
dashboard. One connection serves Enrichment and Output. Input is set up
separately, as a HubSpot source with its own endpoint URL and secret.

Bryn accepts two kinds of HubSpot token, both of which reach your CRM data:

- a **service key** (in HubSpot: **Development**, **Keys**, **Service keys**), or
- a **private app** access token (in HubSpot: **Development**, **Legacy apps**).

A developer API key or a personal access key does not work: they reach
HubSpot's developer tools, not your portal's CRM data, and HubSpot rejects
them as if the token were mistyped. Creating a private app needs a super
admin in HubSpot; a service key needs a super admin or the Developer tools
permission.

<Warning>
**Give the token its CRM scopes.** When you connect, Bryn checks the token
against your HubSpot account details, which needs no CRM scope at all. A
token without the scopes below still connects as verified, but enrichment
finds nothing and Bryn cannot write. Add these scopes when you create it:

- `crm.objects.contacts.read` and `crm.objects.contacts.write`
- `crm.objects.companies.read`
- `crm.objects.deals.read`
- `crm.objects.owners.read` (optional: without it Bryn leaves out the
  owner's name, and every other field still lands)
</Warning>

Bryn keeps the token in encrypted storage; it is never shown again or
logged. You can have one HubSpot connection at a time, since the token
covers your whole portal. To use a different token or portal, disconnect
HubSpot and connect again.

If HubSpot later rejects the token during enrichment (for example because it
was revoked or a scope was removed), Bryn marks the connection as failed so
you can reconnect it under **Integrations**.

## Input

### What arrives

HubSpot sends events to Bryn through a workflow's **Send a webhook** action.
Each workflow enrols one record type, so each one becomes its own Bryn
source:

| Source | Enrols | How Bryn finds the account | How Bryn finds the person |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contact** | Contacts | The contact's primary company | The contact's email |
| **Company** | Companies | The company's own record, joined to a known account by its domain | No person |

Most teams only need a contact source: a contact already carries the company
it belongs to. Add a company source if you also want events about companies
with no contact involved, such as a company becoming a customer.

<Note>
The **Send a webhook** action needs **Data Hub Professional or Enterprise**.
On other HubSpot plans the action shows a padlock.
</Note>

You choose what the event is called by typing a fixed `event` value in the
workflow, so one workflow sends one kind of event. When you create a source,
Bryn proposes two signal rules for it:

| `event` value | Bryn signal | When it fires |
|---|---|---|
| `created` | `crm_contact_created` | The body carries the contact's email |
| `became_customer` | `account_signup` | The body carries the company's domain |

Any other value (for example `became_mql` or `demo_requested`) arrives and is
stored, but raises no signal until you map it under
[Signal mapping](/bryn/signal-mapping). Name the trigger on its own:
`became_mql`, not `contact.became_mql`, since the source name already says
it is a contact.

Bryn also reads the person's first name, last name and job title from a
contact source, and the company's name, industry and number of employees
from a company source. HubSpot's industry values feed
[ICP fit scoring](/bryn/icp-and-scoring). A LinkedIn URL, if you send one, is
kept on the person but not used to work out who they are.

The first event for a HubSpot company Bryn has not linked yet creates a new
account. Once [enrichment](#enrichment) has read that company, later events
join the account Bryn already knows.

### Connect HubSpot as a source

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a HubSpot source in the Bryn dashboard">
    Go to [**Integrations**](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations?view=inputs),
    add **HubSpot** and pick **Contact** or **Company**. Bryn shows the
    endpoint URL and secret for the new source beside the steps below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the workflow in HubSpot">
    Go to **Automation**, **Workflows**, **Create workflow**, **From
    scratch**. Pick the same record type you chose in Bryn (it cannot be
    changed later) and set an enrolment trigger. **Create date is known**
    covers every record, including new ones. A workflow with no trigger
    sends nothing, and nothing warns you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the Send a webhook action">
    You find it under **Data ops**. Set the method to `POST` and paste the
    endpoint URL.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the action's authentication">
    Set **Authentication type** to **API key**, with the key in the request
    header. Create a HubSpot secret named exactly `Authorization` and paste
    the secret value from Bryn into it exactly as shown. HubSpot takes the
    header name from the secret's name, so with any other name Bryn sees no
    credential and refuses every event.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Customize request body and add the keys Bryn reads">
    For a **contact** source:

    - `email`: the contact's **Email**
    - `companyid`: **Record ID** under **Company: Primary** (not **Company:
      Most recently updated**, which changes as records change)
    - `event`: a static value naming the trigger, such as `created`
    - `firstname`, `lastname` and `jobtitle`: **First name**, **Last name**
      and **Job title**
    - `linkedin`: their LinkedIn URL, if you keep one (optional)

    For a **company** source:

    - `companyid`: the company's own **Record ID**
    - `event`: a static value, such as `became_customer`
    - `domain`: **Company Domain Name**, which joins the company to the
      account Bryn already knows
    - `name`: **Name**
    - `industry` and `numberofemployees`: **Industry** and **Number of
      Employees** (both optional)

    Type the keys exactly as shown, in lower case: a key spelled differently
    is silently ignored.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn the workflow on">
    Enrol one record by hand to see an event arrive straight away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Do not choose **Include all properties** for the request body. HubSpot then
sends a much larger body in a different shape, with nothing naming the
event, so Bryn cannot type the event or match it to an account.
</Warning>

**You'll know it worked when** the event shows on the source in the Bryn
dashboard as the source name and your `event` value, for example
`hubspot-contact.created`. An event typed `unmapped` means the `event` key
never arrived: check its spelling in the request body.

### Keeping it working

- **Your own writes can come back.** If Bryn creates or updates a HubSpot
  contact, a workflow triggered on **Create date is known** can enrol it and
  send the change back to Bryn. Keep that in mind before you map such events
  broadly.
- **Re-enrolling raises the signal again.** Enrolling an existing contact by
  hand, to test, raises `crm_contact_created` for a contact that isn't new.
- **Lifecycle stages arrive as HubSpot's internal values** (`lead`, not
  **Lead**), so match those values in your signal rules.

## Enrichment

Bryn looks each person up in your HubSpot by their email address, using an
exact match so a colleague's record is never mistaken for theirs. One lookup
answers at two levels:

- **Account level**: the contact's primary company. Bryn reads its name,
  domain, industry, number of employees, annual revenue, lifecycle stage,
  country and owner, plus the account's open deals: how many there are, and
  the ten most recently updated, each with its name, stage, pipeline, amount,
  close date and owner. A deal counts as open when it is neither closed won
  nor closed lost. Number of employees, country and industry feed
  [ICP fit scoring](/bryn/icp-and-scoring); when you also run Clay, Clay's
  industry wins.
- **Person level**: whether the person is a contact in your HubSpot at all,
  and their job title, contact owner, lifecycle stage and lead status. The
  contact's lifecycle stage is kept apart from the company's, since a lead
  can sit at an account that is already a customer.

Enrichment only fills gaps on the person: a HubSpot job title fills an empty
title and never replaces one from a form or your team. If the person is
removed from HubSpot, Bryn drops their HubSpot details on its next lookup.
When part of HubSpot cannot be read (for example the deal read is refused),
Bryn leaves those fields out rather than reporting zero open deals.

Plays can use these facts, for example whether the person is already in
your CRM, their contact owner, or their lifecycle stage.

**Turn it on.** Connecting HubSpot does not start enrichment by itself. Open
the HubSpot connection under **Integrations** and switch on **Enrichment**.

**When it runs.** Bryn looks HubSpot up when a signal brings an account
forward and the person's email address is known. Accounts Bryn tracked
before you turned enrichment on are read on their next signal. Results are
reused for a day before Bryn asks HubSpot again, and a failed lookup is
retried after an hour.

## Output

The same connection lets Bryn act in your HubSpot. When you connect, your
HubSpot is added to the tools Bryn can use on your behalf, with your token,
so Bryn can work with your HubSpot records, such as creating or updating a
contact. Bryn can only do what the token's scopes allow: leave out
`crm.objects.contacts.write` if you want Bryn to read only.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a token in HubSpot">
    Create a service key, or a private app under **Development**, **Legacy
    apps**, and give it the scopes listed above.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect HubSpot in the Bryn dashboard">
    Go to [**Integrations**](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations?connect=hubspot),
    choose **HubSpot** and paste the token into **Access token**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the connection">
    Bryn verifies the token straight away. If it shows as failed, the token
    was mistyped or revoked, or it is a developer API key or personal access
    key rather than a service key or private app token.
  </Step>
</Steps>
