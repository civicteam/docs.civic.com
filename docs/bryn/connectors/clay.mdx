Clay is a go-to-market data platform with a large database of companies
and people. Bryn uses Clay in one mode, **Enrichment**: when Bryn knows an
account's domain, it looks the company up in Clay and fills in its
firmographics (size, industry, type, country, location, revenue and
funding), which [scoring](/bryn/icp-and-scoring) and your
[Plays](/bryn/plays) then use.

Clay is never a delivery destination. It does not appear in any Play's
outputs, and Bryn writes nothing to your Clay workspace.

## How Bryn integrates Clay

You connect Clay with a Clay API key under [**Integrations**](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations) in the
Bryn dashboard. The key is the only thing Bryn needs: there are no Clay tables,
columns or webhooks to set up, because Bryn calls Clay's company search
directly and reads the answer back in the same request.

<Note>
**Clay runs on your own key and your own Clay plan.** Clay charges per
lookup, so Bryn never lends a shared Clay key. Connecting Clay is the only
way to enrich with it, and every lookup counts against your Clay
workspace's search quota and credits.
</Note>

When you connect, Bryn checks the key by asking Clay who it belongs to.
That check is an identity read, not a search, so it costs nothing. The
connection only counts as verified once Clay accepts the key. Bryn keeps
the key in encrypted storage; it is never shown again, logged, or passed
to Plays.

The connect form also asks whether to **Use Clay for enrichment**. The
switch is on by default, and Bryn starts looking accounts up once the key
is verified. If you turn it off, the connection is saved but no lookups
run until you turn on **Clay enrichment** on the
[Clay card](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations?connector=provider%3Aclay). To stop later, turn that switch off, or disconnect Clay
to remove the key altogether.

To keep spend predictable, Bryn looks each account up at most once every
30 days, including lookups where Clay found nothing.

## Enrichment

Clay enrichment is **account level only**. Bryn asks Clay about the
company behind a domain; it never sends or requests anything about
individual people.

**What Bryn reads.** For a matched company, Bryn keeps the company name,
domain, description, company type, employee size band, country,
location, industry, annual revenue, total funding range, and LinkedIn
URL. Bryn puts these into the account's main fields:

- **Size, type and country** become the account's size band, company type
  and country, which [scoring](/bryn/icp-and-scoring) matches your ICP
  against.
- **Industry** comes from Clay as a LinkedIn industry name, which Bryn
  maps to its standard industry list, so it drives the industry part of
  your Fit score too. Because Clay's industries are more specific than
  most sources, Clay's industry wins when another source (such as your
  CRM or visitor identification) suggests a broader one. A few companies
  still carry an older LinkedIn industry name; Bryn maps those to the
  current one, and drops any it cannot map rather than guess.
- **Revenue and funding** are kept as Clay reports them, alongside the
  other Clay facts.

**Picking the right company.** A domain is often listed by companies that
do not own it: a business whose careers page runs on a hiring platform
lists that platform's domain, and a contractor may list a client's. So
Bryn does not trust Clay's first result. It asks for a few candidates and
only keeps a company whose own website is the domain Bryn asked about (or
the main site of that domain). When several remain, Bryn prefers the
exact match, then the company whose name matches the domain, then one
clearly larger than the rest. If none of these rules settles it, Bryn
keeps no answer rather than pick the wrong company.

When a lookup completes and Clay has no company Bryn will accept, Bryn
removes any earlier Clay answer for that account, so a wrong match does
not linger. When the result is ambiguous, the earlier answer stays as it
is.

**When it runs.** Bryn looks an account up in Clay when the account
produces a new [signal](/bryn/signal-mapping), or when a mapped source
first creates the account. Accounts that existed before you turned Clay
on are enriched on their next signal, not all at once. An account without
a domain is skipped until a domain arrives, and then Clay runs straight
away.

Each account is looked up once per 30 days. A lookup that found nothing
also waits 30 days before Bryn asks again. A lookup that failed waits an
hour before Bryn retries.

**Where results appear.** Open an account in the Bryn dashboard: Clay is
listed under **Enrichment sources** with what it filled and when, and the
fields it filled show on the account. Enrichment activity from Clay is
also in the audit log, where you can filter by the Clay source.

**When Clay refuses.** If Clay rejects the key (for example because it
was revoked), Bryn marks the connection as needing attention on the
[Clay card](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations?connector=provider%3Aclay);
reconnect with a working key to resume. If your Clay workspace runs out of credits, lookups fail and are retried later, so
accounts fill in once you top up. Clay rate limits and brief outages are
retried automatically.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a Clay API key">
    In Clay, open your workspace settings, go to **API and CLI**, then
    **API keys**, and create a key. Make sure the workspace has search
    credits available.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Clay in the Bryn dashboard">
    [Open the Clay connect form](https://bryn.civic.com/web/integrations?connect=clay) (or go to
    **Integrations** and choose **Clay**), paste the key, leave **Use
    Clay for enrichment** on, and connect. Bryn checks the key with Clay
    before saving the connection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that it worked">
    Wait for a new signal on an account that has a domain, then open the
    account. Clay should be listed under **Enrichment sources**, with the
    company's size, industry and country filled in.
  </Step>
</Steps>
