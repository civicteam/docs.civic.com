Apollo is a sales intelligence and engagement platform: a B2B contact and
company database with built-in email sequences. Bryn works with Apollo in
two modes on one API key: **Enrichment**, where Bryn looks up the
companies and people it sees, and **Output**, where a
[Play](/bryn/plays) enrols a person into one of your Apollo sequences.

Bryn uses your own Apollo account, so every lookup and every enrolment
runs on your Apollo credits and plan.

## How Bryn integrates Apollo

You connect Apollo with an API key under **Integrations** in the Bryn
dashboard. One connection serves both modes: the enricher reads person
and account facts with the key, and Plays use the same key to add people
to sequences.

<Warning>
**The kind of API key decides what Bryn can do.** A standard Apollo API
key works for enrichment only. To enrol contacts into sequences, Bryn
needs a **master API key**, or a key scoped to sequences, email accounts
and contacts. If you connect a standard key, enrichment works, but Plays
cannot add anyone to a sequence until you reconnect with a key that has
that access.
</Warning>

When you enter the key, Bryn checks it straight away:

- **A rejected key** shows an error beside the field and you cannot
  connect it.
- **A key without sequence access** shows a note that it still works for
  enrichment. You can connect it for enrichment on its own.
- **A key with sequence access** loads your Apollo sequences and the
  mailboxes you can send from, so you can pick the connection's defaults.

Verifying the connection uses Apollo's account health check, which spends
no credits. Bryn keeps the key in encrypted storage; it is never shown
again, logged, or passed to Plays.

The default sequence and sending mailbox are stored on the connection.
To change them, reconnect Apollo, which checks the key again and offers
both pickers.

## Enrichment

Bryn looks Apollo up at two levels, each shown as its own source in the
Bryn dashboard:

- **Account level (Apollo)**: Bryn looks the company up by its domain and
  only keeps the record when Apollo's primary domain for it matches. It
  reads the company name, description, industry, employee count, annual
  revenue, total funding, founding year, country and location, number of
  retail locations, website, logo, and LinkedIn, X (Twitter) and Facebook
  URLs. Employee count, revenue, funding, country, industry and website
  feed the account's main fields, including its size band, which
  [scoring](/bryn/icp-and-scoring) uses.
- **Person level (Apollo (people))**: Bryn matches one person per lookup,
  using the strongest key it has: a LinkedIn profile URL first, then an
  email address, then a first and last name together with the company's
  domain. A first name alone is never sent, so Apollo cannot match a
  namesake. Bryn reads job title, first and last name, seniority (for
  example `VP` or `Director`), job function, LinkedIn URL, phone number,
  email address and its Apollo status, and employer.

Enrichment only fills gaps. Person details from Apollo fill empty fields
and never replace what a form, your team or a higher-priority source
already set. Bryn only takes an email address from Apollo when Apollo
marks it as verified and the person has none yet; guessed addresses, and
the placeholder Apollo returns when your plan cannot reveal an email, are
never stored as the person's email.

**When it runs.** Bryn looks an account up as soon as it has a domain,
and a person as soon as one of the keys above is known. A person with only
a name waits until the company domain arrives. Results are reused for 30
days before Bryn asks Apollo again.

**When Apollo refuses.** If Apollo rejects the key, Bryn flags the
connection so you can fix it under **Integrations**. If your plan does not
include an endpoint or you run out of credits, Bryn retries within the
hour, so accounts are filled in once you fix the plan.

## Output

A Play can add the person it acts on to an Apollo sequence. Apollo
enrols contacts, so each delivery happens in two steps:

1. Bryn creates the person as an Apollo contact, or finds the existing
   one. Apollo's duplicate check means a repeat delivery never creates a
   second contact or overwrites the one your team already has.
2. Bryn adds that contact to the sequence, sending from the chosen
   mailbox.

Bryn sends the person's email address (required, since sequences email
the contact), and where known their first and last name, job title,
company name, company website and LinkedIn URL.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a master API key in Apollo">
    In Apollo's API settings, create a new key and turn on **Set as
    master key**, or give it access to the sequences, email accounts and
    contacts endpoints. Make sure the Apollo account has at least one
    active mailbox and one sequence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Apollo in the Bryn dashboard">
    Go to **Integrations**, choose **Apollo** and paste the key. Pick a
    **Default sequence** (optional) and the mailbox to **Send from**.
    Apollo's own default mailbox is preselected.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Apollo to a Play">
    In the Play's outputs, add Apollo and pick the connection. Leave the
    sequence and mailbox empty to use the connection's defaults, or pick
    others for this Play. Turn on **Include unverified emails** only if
    you want Apollo to enrol people whose email it has not verified;
    Apollo skips them by default to protect your sending reputation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the entry">
    Click **Test** on the Apollo entry. Bryn checks that the sequence
    still exists and the mailbox is active, without enrolling anyone. If
    the key cannot reach sequences, the test says so and asks for a
    master key.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A connection only counts as an Apollo destination once it is verified and
has a sending mailbox. A connection made with a standard key is
enrichment only, and Plays cannot deliver to it.

**You'll know it worked when** the person appears as a contact in your
Apollo sequence, sending from the mailbox you picked. A person who is
already in that sequence counts as delivered, and is not added twice.

Apollo can also decline a person, for example because they are active in
another sequence or have no verified email. Bryn records that as a
skipped delivery with Apollo's reason, not as a failure. If the mailbox
has since been deactivated in Apollo, the delivery fails; link an active
mailbox in Apollo, then reconnect or pick it in the Play.
